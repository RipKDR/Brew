# Market Positioning Strategy — Brew

> Last updated: April 2026

---

## 1. Positioning Statement

**Brew is the first casual puzzle game where every tap builds something.**

While other games reward you for clearing, sorting, or matching, Brew rewards you for *creating*. Choose your recipe, fuse ingredients into glowing orbs, and brew potions that fill your workshop shelf. The puzzle genre has spent a decade perfecting destruction — Brew introduces creation.

---

## 2. Target Market Size

### Global Mobile Puzzle Market
- **Total revenue:** ~$9.5B annually (2025, Sensor Tower / data.ai)
- **Growth:** 4–6% YoY for the overall category, driven primarily by the top 20 titles
- **Player base:** ~600M monthly active puzzle players worldwide

### Hybrid-Casual Puzzle Segment
- **Segment revenue:** $175M+/month and growing 80%+ YoY (2024–2025)
- **Defining titles:** Pixel Flow, Hexa Sort, Color Block Jam, Block Blast, and a growing long tail
- **Characteristics:** Novel mechanics beyond match-3, casual accessibility, hybrid monetization (IAP + ads), 30–90 second sessions, low CPI relative to match-3

### Brew's Accessible Addressable Market
- **Primary audience:** Puzzle players who actively play but are fatigued by match-3, sort, or merge mechanics — estimated 100M+ potential players globally
- **Demographics:** 25–45, skewing slightly female (60/40), urban and suburban, plays 2–4 puzzle sessions/day, willing to spend $5–20/month on games they enjoy
- **Psychographic:** Values creativity, completionism, cozy aesthetics, fairness in monetization. Likely plays or has played: Cozy Grove, Stardew Valley, Animal Crossing, or Unpacking alongside their mobile puzzle rotation
- **Secondary audience:** Hybrid-casual players who chase novel mechanics — early adopters who drive organic installs and UA creative virality in the first 90 days


### Map interpretation

- **Bottom-left (low novelty, shallow meta):** Aging blast/sort titles with limited retention. Commoditized.
- **Top-left (low novelty, deep meta):** Match-3 incumbents. Retention is meta-driven, not mechanic-driven. Entrenched but vulnerable to novelty fatigue.
- **Bottom-right (high novelty, shallow meta):** Hybrid-casual breakouts. Fast growth, fast churn. Win on mechanic, lose on retention.
- **Top-right (high novelty, deep meta):** The open quadrant. No dominant title occupies this space yet. **This is Brew's target position** — combine genuine mechanic novelty with meaningful meta progression.

Brew's launch position is center-right: high novelty, medium meta. The roadmap moves Brew upward (deeper meta) through workshop expansion, seasonal recipe arcs, and collection systems. The goal is to reach the top-right quadrant within 6 months of global launch.

## 5. Differentiation Framework

### Why players choose Brew over alternatives

| # | Differentiator | What it means for the player | Competitive gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Creation, not destruction** | Every session produces a tangible crafted output — a potion on your shelf. You're building something, not just clearing a board. | No major puzzle competitor frames its core loop as creation. Merge games create items but lack the strategic crafting feel. |
| 2 | **Positional strategy** | WHERE you fuse matters, not just WHAT you fuse. Orb placement changes the board state, creating depth without complexity. | Blast games (Toon Blast, Toy Blast) have no positional decision. Sort games have placement but no growth/chain mechanic. |
| 3 | **Cozy craft theme** | Warm workshop aesthetic, glowing potions, ingredient lore. A world you want to inhabit, not just a board you clear. | Most puzzle games use generic candy, cartoon, or abstract themes. Brew's theme is distinctive and emotionally resonant. |
| 4 | **Fair monetization** | Rewarded-first model: watch an ad for a boost, or buy if you choose. No energy system, no life gates, no aggressive IAP prompts. | Candy Crush and other legacy titles use lives, timers, and aggressive IAP. Brew positions monetization as a feature, not a tax. |
| 5 | **One-tap depth** | Accessible input (tap a cluster) with emergent strategic depth (chain management, orb positioning, recipe targeting). Easy to learn in 30 seconds, satisfying to optimize for months. | Pixel Flow requires dexterity. Match-3 has strategic depth but familiar mechanics. Brew offers both accessibility and novelty. |

### Honest assessment of where Brew must prove itself

| Risk area | Reality check |
|---|---|
| **Meta depth at launch** | Workshop/shelf meta is lighter than Royal Match's castle or Gossip Harbor's narrative at launch. Brew must prove D30+ retention in soft launch and deepen meta aggressively post-launch. |
| **Content volume** | Launching with hundreds of levels vs. incumbents with thousands. The recipe-ingredient system is designed for efficient authoring, but the library takes time. |
| **Brand recognition** | Zero. Brew is unknown. UA creative must do the work that brand awareness does for Candy Crush. The novel mechanic is the primary tool for stopping thumbs in feeds. |
| **Monetization yield** | Rewarded-first is player-friendly but must prove ARPDAU comparable to IAP-heavy competitors. Soft launch will calibrate the balance. |

## 6. Competitive Moats (Planned)

| Moat | Timeline | Mechanism |

## 7. Go-to-Market Timeline

### Phase 1: Soft Launch (Weeks 1–8)

**Markets:** Canada, Australia, Philippines
- Canada: high-value, English-speaking, representative of US behavior
- Australia: similar profile, different timezone for coverage
- Philippines: high-volume, tests retention and ad monetization at scale

**Objectives:**
- Validate tutorial completion rate (target: 85%+)
- Measure D1 retention (target: 45%+), D7 (target: 20%+), D30 (target: 8%+)
- Test CPI across 3 UA creative themes (mechanic demo, ASMR craft, fail/retry humor)
- Calibrate IAP and rewarded-ad revenue balance (target ARPDAU: $0.08+ blended)
- Identify first retention cliff and iterate

**Kill criteria:** If D1 < 35% or CPI > $3.00 after creative iteration, reassess core mechanic or theme before scaling.

### Phase 2: Metrics Evaluation (Weeks 6–10, overlapping)

**Review cadence:** Weekly metrics review, bi-weekly deep dives.

| Metric | Soft launch target | Global launch threshold |
|---|---|---|
| D1 retention | 45%+ | 40%+ |
| D7 retention | 20%+ | 18%+ |
| D30 retention | 8%+ | 7%+ |
| Tutorial completion | 85%+ | 80%+ |
| CPI (US equivalent) | < $2.50 | < $3.00 |
| ARPDAU (blended) | $0.08+ | $0.06+ |
| Session length | 6+ min | 5+ min |
| Sessions/day | 3+ | 2.5+ |

### Phase 3: Global Launch

**Trigger:** Soft launch metrics pass thresholds for 2+ consecutive weeks after stabilization.

**Launch markets:** US, UK, Germany, Japan, South Korea, Brazil — phased over 2 weeks to manage server load and UA budget.

**Launch UA strategy:**
- Lead with mechanic-demo creatives (show fusion-chain-brew in action — the novel mechanic is the hook)
- Secondary: ASMR potion-brewing satisfaction videos
- Tertiary: fail/retry humor ("I almost brewed it!")
- Channels: Meta (Facebook/Instagram), TikTok, Google UAC, Apple Search Ads, ironSource/Unity Ads network

### Phase 4: Post-Launch Growth (Months 2–6)

**Content cadence:**
- Weekly: 20–30 new levels, minor balance tuning
- Bi-weekly: New recipe set introduction
- Monthly: Content arc (new ingredient theme, workshop expansion area, limited-edition potion collection)
- Seasonal: Major themed events (4 per year) with exclusive recipes and workshop decorations

**Feature roadmap highlights:**
- Month 2: Social features (friend shelves, potion gifting)
- Month 3: Competitive events (weekly brew-offs, leaderboards)
- Month 4: Workshop customization (shelf styles, ambient effects)
- Month 5: Collaborative brewing (guild/team events)
- Month 6: Second meta layer evaluation (narrative, collection, or competitive — based on data)

## 9. Positioning Summary

Brew occupies a distinctive position in the mobile puzzle market:

- **It is not a match-3 game** — it avoids the saturated CPI battlefield where Royal Match and Candy Crush dominate through budget.
- **It is not a pure hybrid-casual novelty** — it has meta depth and retention design that sort and merge games lack.
- **It is a creation game in a destruction category** — the emotional frame is unique and resonant.
- **It is strategically deep but casually accessible** — positional play adds depth without adding complexity to the input.

The competitive landscape is large, well-funded, and entrenched. Brew does not win by outspending incumbents. It wins by being *genuinely different* in a category where players are hungry for something new — and by executing that difference with polish, fairness, and a theme that makes every session feel like you built something worth keeping.
